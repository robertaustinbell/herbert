# Herbert's intellectual lineage and adaptation

Herbert synthesizes ideas rather than reproducing any one author's system. The name acknowledges Herbert Simon's work on bounded rationality, attention, decision-making, and complex systems. It does not imply that this project reproduces Simon's identity, endorses every conclusion he reached, or speaks for him.

Important influences include:

- Jonathan Baron — active open-minded thinking, search, inference, and decision quality
- Ken Binmore — model adequacy and limits on strategic formalization
- Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky — judgment under uncertainty, representation effects, and outcome bias
- Herbert Simon — bounded rationality and attention as a scarce resource
- G. K. Chesterton — understand a structure's function before removing it
- Charles Goodhart — objectives must outrank optimized proxies
- W. Edwards Deming — quality must be built into the operating system, not inspected in afterward
- Donella Meadows — feedback loops, information flows, constraints, and leverage points
- Philip Tetlock — calibration, prediction, and genuine belief revision
- Nassim Nicholas Taleb — asymmetric downside, optionality, robustness, and the adviser's lack of skin in the game
- Gautam Baid — bounded translations of marginal reinvestment economics, current-alternative comparison, and prompt disposition of contrary evidence; the source's investment heuristics and hero-outcome examples are not universalized
- Rui Meng and the ScientistOne collaborators — claim-level evidence linkage and separate completeness, correctness, specification, and method–artifact audits; adapted here as a bounded candidate rather than a claim of general proof

These names are provenance, not appeals to authority. The operating thought is intentionally bounded, revisable, and sometimes in tension with the authors themselves. Consult original works for their actual arguments.

The public starter was adapted from Bobert's privately developed architecture. Personal identity, private continuity, domain records, operational history, and private evidence were excluded by design.

## Selected source references

The following works informed selected concepts in [Strategic Response and Incentives](operating-thought/decisions/strategic-response-and-incentives.md). These are bounded source attributions, not claims that the authors endorse Herbert or that the adapted prompts are empirically validated.

- Gabriel Weinberg and Lauren McCann, [*Super Thinking: The Big Book of Mental Models*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/562923/super-thinking-by-gabriel-weinberg-and-lauren-mccann/) (Portfolio, 2019). Selected sections: “Social Norms vs. Market Norms,” “Secret Sauce,” and “Vision Without Execution Is Just Hallucination.” The strategic prompts are local adaptations, not the authors' prescribed procedure; underlying attributions in the book were not independently reviewed.
- Steven Tadelis, [*Game Theory: An Introduction*](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691129082/game-theory) (Princeton University Press, 2013). Selected passages: ch. 3; ch. 4 §4.3; ch. 8 §§8.1, 8.3; ch. 12 §12.3; ch. 13 §13.2; and ch. 17 §17.1. These provide context for selected game-model concepts; they do not make strategic predictions certain or validate the page's separate integrity and authority rules.
